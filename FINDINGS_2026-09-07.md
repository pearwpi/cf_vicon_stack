# Findings — 2026-09-07/08, lab session on pear-2

Six hours of measurement. Three things that were believed turned out to be
wrong, one mechanism was identified directly for the first time, and three
instruments were found to be reporting health over broken state.

The through-line: **every failure in this session was silent.** Nothing
crashed. Every status command said fine. The failures were only visible to
something that measured the thing itself rather than a proxy for it.

---

## 1. The Vicon link was losing 200 ms of pose data at a time

`vicon-rto.service` had reported `active (exited)` continuously since the
Sep 5 boot, over a route that **did not exist**.

    ExecStart=/usr/sbin/ip route replace 192.168.10.1/32 dev enp4s0 rto_min 20ms

The unit installs a /32 host route. NetworkManager re-applies `enp4s0`'s
configuration on carrier changes, DHCP renewals and `reapply`, flushing the
interface's routes and taking the hand-added /32 with it. `RemainAfterExit=yes`
means systemd never looks again. The route was gone; the unit still said fine.

The socket was the only honest witness:

    rto:202  cwnd:3  ssthresh:2  retrans:0/120  minrtt:0.049
    bytes_received:122 MB   bytes_sent:2.8 MB

`rto:202` — the retransmission timeout was Linux's 200 ms default, not 20 ms.

**Why each loss cost the full 200 ms.** Data flows one way: Vicon streams to
pear-2 (122 MB in against 2.8 MB out). Our retransmissions are of our own
small, sparse request packets. A sparse sender cannot trigger fast retransmit,
which needs three duplicate ACKs and therefore following data in flight, so
recovery falls back to the RTO timer every time. `cwnd:3 ssthresh:2` on a link
with `minrtt` of 49 µs is a connection that has been beaten flat by loss.

**Measured effect of the fix** (same rig, minutes apart):

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| drop burst | 48–49 frames | 4 frames |
| hole duration | 200 ms | 16.7 ms |
| total loss | 289 / 15600 = 1.85% | 76 / 34800 = 0.22% |

An 8.5× cut in lost data, and the worst hole moved from well over
`pose_stale_s` (150 ms) to well under it.

**What the fix did not do.** The *event* rate barely changed — roughly one
loss event per 10 s before, one per 7 s after. The link loses packets just as
often; each loss is now 12× cheaper. Around 0.7 losses/second on a dedicated
point-to-point link is high and still wants a look at cable, switch port and
NIC offload. It is no longer flight-critical, which is different from fixed.

**Persistence is unresolved.** NetworkManager 1.36.6 rejects `rto_min` as a
route attribute, so NM cannot own the route. A dispatcher hook at
`/etc/NetworkManager/dispatcher.d/90-vicon-rto` covers `up`, `down` and DHCP
changes — verified working when invoked. It does **not** cover `reapply`: NM
1.36.6 emits no dispatcher event for it, confirmed by
`NetworkManager-dispatcher.service` having last activated hours earlier.

The conclusion to carry forward is not "make the route immortal". This route
vanished twice while every status command reported health. **The protection
that matters is refusing to fly without checking** — see §7.

## 2. Vicon Tracker runs at 240.0 Hz

Settled to four significant figures, by a method that needs no configuration
screen. The bridge logs every 1200 *received* frames, so the wall time between
log lines should be `(1200 + dropped) / rate`:

| Δt between log lines | frames dropped | (1200 + dropped) / 240 |
|---|---|---|
| 5.000, 5.001, 4.999 | 0 | 5.000 s |
| 5.017 | 4 | 5.017 s |
| 5.033 | 8 | 5.033 s |
| 5.050 | 12 | 5.050 s |
| 5.197 | 47 | 5.196 s |
| 5.396 | 95 | 5.396 s |

Every row matches to the millisecond, across two different loss regimes. The
open "120 vs 240 Hz" question from §2 of the earlier findings is closed: 240.

## 4. The `crazyflie2` template is rotationally ambiguous

**This supersedes the mechanism proposed in Findings 1.7.**

Every yaw flip observed under controlled conditions, with all seven markers
visible and no data gap:

| dt (ms) | d_yaw | d_att | pitch | roll | markers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.3 | −85.61 | 125.91 | −39.92 | 154.34 | 7/7 |
| 4.1 | 85.01 | 126.22 | −3.90 | 4.97 | 7/7 |
| 4.4 | 86.37 | 86.43 | −5.11 | 3.81 | 7/7 |
| 4.4 | −85.54 | 85.56 | −4.94 | 3.65 | 7/7 |
| 4.3 | −94.61 | 159.29 | 0.22 | 162.18 | 7/7 |
| 3.9 | 94.29 | 158.30 | −8.95 | 4.90 | 7/7 |
| 4.4 | −94.47 | 159.27 | 0.19 | 161.07 | 7/7 |
| 3.9 | 94.70 | 160.28 | −6.38 | 3.64 | 7/7 |

Three candidate explanations were on the table. The data eliminates two:

- **Not a data gap.** Every `dt` is one frame at 240 Hz (3.9–4.4 ms), and
  receipt gaps across the run were median 4.20 ms, p99.9 6.90 ms. An 86–160°
  attitude change in 4 ms is 21,000–40,000 deg/s. No hand does that.
- **Not the yaw parameterisation.** `d_att` is the geodesic angle between
  consecutive quaternions and has no singularity. For comparison, the
  parameterisation artefact was captured separately in the same session:
  at pitch 88.06°, a **1.08° body motion produced a 29.21° yaw swing**.
  That is what the artefact looks like. These are not it.
- **Not occlusion.** 7 of 7 markers throughout, zero partial frames.

The flips occur in **pairs that go out and return**, usually within one frame,
and the `roll` column shows the body reported inverted (154–162°) for that
single frame before snapping back. The magnitudes fall into two families,
**~86–95°** and **~158–160°**, which is exactly the 90°/180° competitor
structure `marker_geom.py` predicted offline from the template geometry.

**Consequence for the hardware fix.** The centre post is still correct, but
for a different reason and with a different acceptance test. The problem is
not keeping markers visible; it is that a nearly-coplanar seven-marker set
admits 90° and 180° rotations that map it onto itself within fit tolerance.
Raising one marker out of plane destroys both families at once.

- **Old gate:** `marker_geom.py --vsk` double-dropout residual ≥ 8 mm.
- **New gate:** the best non-identity competitor on the **full** seven-marker
  set, plus out-of-plane depth as a fraction of the spread. Thresholds are now
  set from this measurement rather than guessed: the layout scores 25.2 mm and
  0.096, and it demonstrably flips, so both must be inside the failure region.

**And the intended fix was wrong.** Simulating it in `marker_geom.py`, adding
an eighth marker on a centre post makes the layout *worse* — 25.2 mm down to
13.3 mm at a 30 mm height — because a point on the axis of the competing
rotations is very nearly invariant under them: it adds almost no discriminating
information and brings new small-angle near-symmetries with it.

Raising an **existing** marker works. `crazyflie22` at 28.6 mm radius takes the
margin to 41.2 mm at +35 mm and improves monotonically with height, so a few
millimetres of build error stays safe. `crazyflie27` scores higher (45.5 mm)
but collapses to 17.3 mm at +20 mm and sits far out on an arm, where the mass
costs inertia and the post is closer to the props. Run
`marker_geom.py --vsk crazyflie2.vsk --sweep` for the full table.

These remain model numbers. The acceptance test is empirical: build it,
re-teach the object, fly, and watch `track_monitor.py`.

Occlusion is still real — a 1.0% partial-frame rate was seen in one run — but
it is not what produced these flips, and the fix should be judged on ambiguity.

## 5. Three instruments were reporting health over broken state

The pattern is worth naming, because it recurred three times in one session:
**each measurement was invisible in exactly the configuration it was tested in.**

**5.1 `track_monitor.py` measured yaw, not orientation.** Yaw is a *parameter*
of an orientation, ill-conditioned near pitch ±90°. Its first real run
reported 88 flips, 79 of them "clean", and concluded there were two problems.
That conclusion was unsupported: the run was hand-held through steep
attitudes, precisely where the artefact lives, and the tool could not tell a
parameterisation blow-up from a solver branch jump. Fixed by logging roll,
pitch and the geodesic `d_att`, and classifying each flip REAL or PARAM.

**5.2 The occlusion attribution window was 200 ms.** When Vicon loses markers
it can settle into a wrong branch and *stay* there; the visible jump comes on
recovery, possibly seconds later. A 200 ms boolean scores those as "clean".
The 9-of-88 split from the first run should not be trusted.

**5.3 `TransformedPoseSource` dropped `capture_t_s`.** It rebuilt the
`PoseSample` without the capture stamp, so `latency_s` became NaN the moment a
calibration transform entered the path — that is, in every real HITL run, and
in none of the fake-room dry runs it was tested with. Fixed, with a regression
test verified to fail without the fix.

## 6. The Docker build had been broken for six days, silently

Two fixes from Sep 1 contradicted each other four hours apart:

- **16:46** `fix_docker.py` added a Dockerfile guard asserting
  `src/crazyflie_ros/crazyflie_ros/cf_core.py` is a **symlink**.
- **17:42** `fix_symlinks.py` converted exactly those symlinks into **real
  files**, because symlinks do not survive a USB transfer, and moved the
  anti-drift guarantee to `DUPLICATES.json` + `test_duplicates.py`.

Every `docker compose build` since has died at that guard. The image therefore
froze at the Aug 28 source, and the Sep 1 track-quality patch — the marker
telemetry that this whole investigation depends on — **had never once run**.

Five sites asserted the stale invariant: the Dockerfile guard,
`test_crazyflie_ros.py` (two `os.path.realpath` checks, the two that failed the
build), `docker/verify.py`, `README.md` and `docker/DOCKER.md`. All five now
check byte identity instead of link resolution, which passes under either
arrangement.

The lesson is about *shape*: the guards were right about the danger and wrong
about the mechanism, and nothing tied the two fixes together.

## 7. What this argues for next

A preflight check, run before every session, that measures the things
themselves rather than their proxies:

- `rto:` read off the live socket to the Vicon server — not `systemctl
  status`, not `ip route show`
- pose publish rate at the subscriber
- marker completeness over a sample window
- `now − header.stamp` latency
- zero `d_att` branch jumps while the drone sits still

Every item on that list corresponds to something that lied to us tonight.

## 8. Open

- **Controlled occlusion, with tape not fingers.** A fingertip does not remove
  a marker from a multi-camera volume — the attempted test recorded zero
  partial frames. Opaque tape over one marker removes it from every camera;
  the live line should read 6/7 before the run starts.
- **`marker_geom.py` on the full seven-marker set** — the new acceptance gate.
- **The centre post itself**, 30–35 mm, avoid 40 mm; then delete and re-teach
  the `crazyflie2` object in Tracker.
- **`--yaw-sign` verification, props off.**
- **The underlying packet loss.** The RTO fix made each loss 12× cheaper and
  did not reduce how often they happen.
- **`reapply` remains an uncovered path** for the RTO route.
